The Context

During my internship at a wellness startup, I noticed daily attendance metrics were consistently low during a festive week. Since attendance directly affected engagement and renewal rates, this was a serious risk.

The Ownership

My manager was on leave, but instead of waiting, I proactively flagged the issue and collaborated with a senior team member. We analyzed patterns and designed a short-term incentive program aimed at habit reactivation rather than just discounts.

The Outcome

This initiative stabilized attendance during the low-engagement period and prevented a drop in renewals. It taught me when to act first and align later, especially when data signals immediate risk.

The Context

During my startup experience, I personally interviewed 20+ professors and PhD students across Tier-1 institutions to validate our ecosystem hypothesis.

The Insight

Initially, we assumed that the main problem was the lack of a dedicated community for researchers. However, after deeper exploration and user conversations, we realized this was not the real problem. We then started classifying our target users based on how early or mature they were in their research careers. This shift helped us uncover a more pressing issue. For users at an early stage, the biggest challenge was not community—but the highly disorganized process of finding and applying for research internships.

The Pivot

Based on this insight, we narrowed our focus to early-stage researchers. We began by forming a small WhatsApp community and manually curating research internship opportunities by actively searching the internet and sharing relevant openings. This helped us validate the problem quickly and understand user needs before building anything at scale.

The Situation

I was optimizing a landing page for an influencer-led wellness brand. I applied industry "best practices"—value-led messaging and benefits-led hierarchy—expecting a significant CTR lift.

The Failure

After launch, CTR didn't budge. A root cause analysis showed the flaw: for this specific brand, users cared more about the influencer's social proof and personal presence than the standalone product benefits.

The Correction

We pivoted the hierarchy to lead with social proof and the influencer narrative. This experience taught me that "best practices" only work when they match the brand's unique trust model.

Pulse Browser

Intent-based AI browsing designed to reduce cognitive overload and preserve intent.

Concept-Driven Product Case Study

Category

Product Strategy

View Strategy Deck

Problem

Existing workflows are broken by tab explosion and intent loss. Always-on AI browsers often worsen this by over-automating without trust, causing users to lose track of their original research intent.

Who It’s For

Professionals and students who need high-intent control over their digital workspace without being overwhelmed by constant AI interference.

Core Insight

Productivity is about reducing the friction between user intent and digital action. Every automated step must be intentional, not forced. The browser should act as a semantic memory layer rather than just a tab-rendering engine.

Solution Overview

A dual-mode browser featuring Normal vs AI toggles. Key innovations include a Semantic Tab Finder (task-based retrieval), Smart Cursor (hover summaries), and a Tasks/Projects sidebar for session continuity.

MVP Decisions

Focused on RICE-prioritized features like Dual-Mode Toggle and task-based semantic memory instead of raw tab history to ensure privacy and low cognitive load.

Metrics

North Star: Session Context Continuity. Secondary: Reduction in tab-switch frequency and adoption rate of explicit AI features.

Risks & Mitigations

User hesitation around AI; mitigated by intent-based triggers and a transparent, privacy-first GTM strategy.

What This Demonstrates

Strategic feature prioritization using RICE, behavioural psychology in UI design, and an intent-centric approach to AI integration.

Active Stillness

Reframing boredom as a state of mental depth and creative reflection.

Concept-Driven Product Case Study

Category

Behavioral Strategy

View Strategy Deck

Problem

The total absence of boredom due to constant digital micro-stimulation is destroying deep focus and essential reflection. Modern users face attention decay and creative burnout.

Who It’s For

Modern digital users facing attention spans decay, emotional fatigue, and a loss of creative depth.

Core Insight

Boredom is not empty time; it is a productive state of mental under-stimulation that enables essential reflection and original insight. Reclaiming it requires a structured "off-boarding" from digital noise.

Solution Overview

A guided, session-based experience with a transition into stillness (grounding) followed by discussion-driven reflection using open questions rather than answers.

MVP & Scope

Focused on experience over content quantity. Prioritized a progressive reduction of guidance to build user habit formation without engagement hacks.

Metrics

North Star: Voluntary Return Rate. Key indicators: Stillness Onset Time and Focus Stability Duration during the session lifecycle.

Risks & Mitigations

Dependency: Mitigated by reducing guidance over time. Exhaustion: Mitigated by session-duration caps.

What This Demonstrates

First-principles thinking applied to counter-intuitive behavioral problems and the ability to design systems for habit formation without engagement hacks.

Echo.ai

Context-aware AI meeting assistant focused on trust-first design.

Working Prototype Details

Category

Working Prototype

View Prototype

Problem

Existing tools act as intrusive bots, causing distraction and lack of trust during privacy-sensitive discussions. Users often feel a loss of context and struggle with unclear mental models of the AI's role.

Who It’s For

Knowledge workers and product teams requiring high-speed decision tracking without distracting visible bots.

Value Proposition

Echo.ai is a trust-first assistant that quietly preserves decisions and follow-ups. It follows a Before–During–After lifecycle to ensure continuity without acting as an intrusive "participant."

Design Principles

Predictability over surprise. Transparency over automation magic. User control, privacy, and continuity. Calm visuals and objection handling via friction-reducing microcopy.

Impact

How building a clear mental model and reducing friction via microcopy can build confidence in AI tools, especially in knowledge-intensive workflows.

Doctor Management Tool

Consultation-centric MVP designed to reduce cognitive load for specialists.

Working Prototype Details

Category

B2B SaaS MVP

View Prototype

Problem

Medical specialists (e.g., gynecologists) face scattered patient history and repetitive data-entry, which increases cognitive load and slows down consultations during high-volume hours.

Value Proposition

A thread-based patient model that treats every visit as a separate visit block while maintaining a continuous historical thread. Focuses on speed-of-access for clinicians over raw data quantity.

How It Works

Doctor-first system where every consultation is captured in discrete visit blocks. Patients provide structured inputs, but doctors retain full clinical control over the final PRES (prescriptions) and historical context.

Principles

Doctor-first clinical control. Continuous context preservation. Decision-speed over over-automation. Privacy-centric data architecture.

What This Demonstrates

Effective SaaS architecture for high-velocity medical workflows, prioritizing clinician decision-speed and safety through focused usable design.
